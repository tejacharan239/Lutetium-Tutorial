Where this sits for a grade 1, stage 4 NET

Lu‑177 DOTATATE is not usually the first thing tried in grade 1 disease. A slow, somatostatin‑receptor‑positive tumour typically starts on a long‑acting somatostatin analogue, which controls hormone symptoms and slows growth. PRRT enters the picture when that stops working.

Step 1

Somatostatin analogue. Octreotide LAR or lanreotide. Symptom control plus an antiproliferative effect.

Step 2

Documented progression. Radiological progression on SSA is the usual trigger to escalate.

Step 3

Ga-68 DOTATATE PET. Confirms the target exists and is widespread enough to be worth irradiating.

Step 4

Four cycles of PRRT. With SSA continued between cycles and afterwards.

What this page simplifies

Three honest caveats. First, NETTER‑1 enrolled midgut tumours with Ki‑67 up to 20%, so it is a grade 1 and grade 2 population — the trial was not restricted to grade 1, and results for pancreatic primaries come from other datasets. Second, "grade 1, stage 4" is not by itself an indication: progression, receptor positivity, adequate marrow and kidney function, and performance status all gate the decision. Third, dosimetry is personalised in many centres rather than fixed at four flat doses, and retreatment after progression is an active question rather than settled practice.

Terms used above

PRRT
Peptide receptor radionuclide therapy — a targeting peptide carrying a therapeutic radionuclide.
SSTR2
Somatostatin receptor subtype 2, the surface protein overexpressed on well-differentiated NETs.
DOTA
A macrocyclic chelator whose four arms grip the metal ion so it cannot dissociate in circulation.
Ki-67
The fraction of tumour cells staining for a proliferation marker; the basis of NET grading.
Krenning score
A 0–4 visual grading of tracer uptake relative to normal liver and spleen.
Crossfire
Irradiation of cells adjacent to a loaded cell, because the β⁻ range exceeds one cell diameter.
GBq
Gigabecquerel, a billion decays per second — the unit the dose is prescribed in.
5-HIAA
A serotonin metabolite measured in urine, used as a tumour marker in midgut NETs.
